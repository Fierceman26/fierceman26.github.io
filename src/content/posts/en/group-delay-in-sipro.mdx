> A working note. The conclusions may not travel, but every step was run on a real design.

Lately I have been tuning group delay on a DDR5-6400 channel. The delay difference inside the differential pair sits around **18 ps**.
By the usual accounting, that has already eaten a third of the eye margin. What SIPro gives you is a view at the level of network parameters.
You can see, on the layout, which segment contributed how much. How to actually "tune" it is left to you.

Below is a comparison of the paths from this pass — not a tutorial. Closer to a work log.

## Start by measuring

Every optimization starts with a measurement. The group-delay view in SIPro does not lie,
but it is easy to **misread the unit**. Read group delay along the frequency axis and the curve itself is on the order of nanoseconds.
The skew you care about is the **difference** between the two lines of the pair, and that difference jitters on the order of picoseconds.

```python
# Pull group-delay data from ADS Python
from keysight.ads import datalink

ds = datalink.open("path/to/sim.ds")
gd_p = ds.get_named("GD(S(2,1))")   # positive leg
gd_n = ds.get_named("GD(S(4,3))")   # negative leg
skew = (gd_p - gd_n) * 1e12          # to ps
print(f"max intra-pair skew = {skew.abs().max():.2f} ps")
```

This step produces the **objective**. Every change after it is an attempt to make that number smaller.

## Path A: length tuning

The classic move. Add a length-tuning serpentine in SIPro
and bring the short leg up to the length of the long one.

In practice:

- **The length you add** scales with `√(L · C)`, but the "length" SIPro sees on the layout
  is geometric length, not electrical length. The difference comes from Dk, copper thickness, and the reference-layer stack.
- In the breakout at the BGA, **length matching inside the fanout** is the hardest part — the traces change layers,
  and the Dk is not the same. Forcing the match on the main signal layer distorts the differential mode.

This time I changed the approach inside the fanout. **Both legs drop symmetrically through the same via stub**,
and I did not compensate length on the main signal layer. Intra-pair skew fell from 18 ps to about 9 ps.

## Path B: a consistent reference

A quieter source — **a broken reference**.

On a DDR5 channel we regularly cross a split plane.
DQ and DQS, in particular, leave some BGA balls and borrow a stretch that has no solid reference.
Group delay then wobbles in a way that is hard to see by eye as "it is here."

I used a crude method that worked. On SIPro's net visualization I **highlighted the two legs separately**
and checked whether each segment saw the same reference net.
The moment the two legs cite different pieces of stitched GND on different segments, they will contribute skew.

The fix: change the BGA pin order so both legs see the same unbroken plane on every segment.

## Path C: symmetry of the via stub

A via is small, but the delay difference from an **asymmetric stub** can kill the signal above 6 GHz.

```text
Stubs on the two legs of the pair:
  positive:  drilled-thru, stub 5.2 mil
  negative:  back-drilled, stub 0.8 mil
```

That structural asymmetry does not explode the group delay in SIPro immediately. SIPro solves
linear network parameters. A stub brings in a high-frequency resonance,
and the visible damage to group delay sits at a higher band.

**Conclusion:** before you tune intra-pair skew, check whether the via stubs were treated the same way. The same treatment (both back-drilled, or both thru)
matters more than "which one is shorter."

## How you know a change actually worked

One group-delay run in SIPro is not enough. You have to feed the S-parameters back into a channel simulation
and look at the eye. SIPro gives you the channel itself. The eye is the channel plus the driver, the receiver, and the equalization.

What I do is run a channel simulation on every revision of the design
and write down three numbers:

| Revision | intra-pair skew | eye height | eye width |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | 18 ps | 38 mV | 0.51 UI |
| Path A | 9 ps  | 51 mV | 0.58 UI |
| A + B  | 5 ps  | 58 mV | 0.61 UI |
| A + B + C | 4 ps | 60 mV | 0.62 UI |

On this revision, path C **paid the least**. When the PVT corners are all run,
the benefit of C shows up on the worst case. It is a slow benefit.

---

## A few rules I kept

1. **Do not length-match first.** Make the reference consistent first, make the vias symmetric first, and length-match last.
   Length matching is compensatory. It spends a lot of layout area, and it moves around at the PVT corners.
2. **Every change goes through channel sim.** The SIPro view can flatter you. The number is real only after it is put back into the channel.
3. **A flat group delay is not a small skew.** Skew is the difference inside the pair. Flat only means one leg looks tidy on its own.

Next time I want to try a sweep with SIPro's net parametrization — let the length-tuning
length move on its own, and watch the skew curve that comes out of channel sim. If that run closes, it gets a note of its own.
