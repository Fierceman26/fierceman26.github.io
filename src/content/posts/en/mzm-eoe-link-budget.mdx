Pluggable optical modules in the data center are giving way to **optical I/O** — the signal leaves the ASIC and is modulated directly,
then travels as light. In that architecture the EOE budget (electrical → optical → electrical) is much tighter than a conventional electrical link,
because **nonlinearity at the optical end, detector noise, and the limited SNR of an analog receive front end**
stack together, and there is not much room left for digital equalization.

Below are a few terms I keep dropping when I run a budget in the ADS Photonics Designer Suite.

## 1. Process drift of the MZM bias point

In theory an MZM sits at the quadrature bias point (the Q-point), where the small-signal linearity is best.
In practice the bias point drifts with temperature, wavelength, and the modulator's own thermo-optic coefficient.

If ADS is given only an ideal MZM, the budget comes out **2–3 dB** too optimistic.
In OMA (optical modulation amplitude) that is a 30–50% loss.

The right way to do this in a system simulation:

- Give the MZM a **bias control loop** model, even a simplified first-order loop
- In a corner run, sweep the bias error out to about ±5°

## 2. TIA input-referred noise

The receiver is a photodetector plus a TIA. Budget tables often count only the PD's shot noise
and **leave out the TIA's input-referred current noise**.

Typical numbers:

| Contributor | Noise (in a 16 GHz bandwidth) |
| --- | --- |
| PD shot noise (1 mA photocurrent) | ~22 nA RMS |
| TIA input current noise | ~80 nA RMS |
| Total | ~83 nA RMS |

Which is to say **the TIA is the dominant noise source**, not the PD. The budget has to include it.

## 3. Retiming jitter → timing noise

If the receiver has a CDR, the BER after retiming is not only the voltage noise of the eye.
It is also the jitter of the **sampling instant**.

In the ADS envelope simulator, above a 30 GHz baud rate, CDR jitter
is usually on the order of 0.5–1 ps RMS. On a UI whose eye is 16 ps wide, that is **3–6%**,
and it comes straight out of the timing margin.

> A rule of thumb: at a BER target of 1e-12, leave jitter its own 6 dB of budget. That is easier than discovering, afterwards, that there was not enough.

## A conservative budget template

The EOE budget I use now looks roughly like this, in dB:

```text
Transmitter
  Laser RIN ............... 1.5
  MZM ER (extinction) ..... 6.0  (10 dB ER)
  MZM bias drift .......... 2.0  (PVT corner)
  Driver linearity ........ 0.8

Channel
  Fiber attenuation ....... 0.3  (50 m)
  Connector losses ........ 1.5  (3 × 0.5 dB)

Receiver
  PD responsivity ......... 0
  TIA input noise ......... 4.0  (the dominant term)
  TIA bandwidth ........... 1.2
  Retiming jitter ......... 6.0

Margins
  Aging + temperature .... 3.0
  Manufacturing tolerance . 2.0
  ──────────────────────────────
  Total budget ............ 28.3 dB
```

Take that number off the OMA you actually have, and what remains is the window in which the link can close an eye and stay closed.

An optical I/O budget is tight not because any single term is terrible, but because **none of them can be skipped**.
