220 nm SOI, a strip 500 nm wide, fully etched, buried in oxide, wavelength 1550 nm.
I ran FDE on that cross-section in Ansys Lumerical MODE, and the solver handed back three modes.
The first two are guided. The third has an neff stuck to the silica, and about −1.5 dB/cm of negative loss.
**That is not a guided mode.**

Propagation is along z. The cross-section is xy. The core is `Si (Silicon) - Palik`, the background is `SiO2 (Glass) - Palik`.
Three objects in the layout: the waveguide `Si_wg`, the solve region `FDE`, and a mesh override `wg_mesh` near the waveguide.
The FDE window is about 4 µm, eight trial modes, a global mesh of dx = dy = 20 nm, PML on all four sides.
`wg_mesh` pulls the mesh near the waveguide down to 5 nm.

## The three solutions

| Mode | neff | TE fraction | loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mode1, TE0 | 2.445347 | 0.9835 | ~ 0 |
| mode2, TM0 | 1.771059 | 0.0444 | ~ 0 |
| mode3 | 1.492897 | ≈ 0.70 | ≈ −1.5 dB/cm |

A TE fraction near 1 is TE. Near 0 is TM. 0.9835 and 0.0444 are clean on both sides.

On this same 500 nm strip, TE is bound more tightly than TM, so TE0 has the higher neff and a narrower |E|².
The two figures are that difference.

The frame for this comparison comes from Lukas Chrostowski and Michael Hochberg, *Silicon Photonics Design: From Devices to Systems* (Cambridge, 2015). Chapter 2 uses FDE on waveguides that are uniform along the direction of propagation. Chapter 3 compares how TE and TM are bound in a strip. The book supplies only that frame. The neff, the polarization fractions, and the field plots above are from this simulation.

## The one stuck to the cladding index

mode3 has neff = 1.492897, close to `n_SiO2` ≈ 1.44.
The loss is about −1.5 dB/cm, and the sign is backwards: the solver is reporting gain.
That is a numerical artifact of a radiating field meeting the PML, not the waveguide amplifying light.
The TE fraction is ≈ 0.70, so the polarization is not clean either. Throw it out.

Three roots were found among eight trial modes. Remove this one and the guided modes left are TE0 and TM0.
So at 1550 nm this 500 nm strip is single-moded for both TE and TM.

The first version of the script saved the `.lms` before Find Modes was clicked.
Open the file and all you see is the layout: `Si_wg`, the FDE window, the mesh.
The mode fields appear only after you click Find Modes again in the GUI.
That is useful while you are learning the interface. It is not a simulation result.
