Almost every power-integrity design starts from this formula:

```text
              V_ripple
Z_target  =  ─────────
             I_transient
```

It is simple to write down, and it is regularly misread — take two numbers off the ASIC datasheet, divide, done.
This note is about two common misreadings, and what they cost.

## Misreading 1: `I_transient` is not the average current

`I_max` on a datasheet is usually a peak of the average. **It is not the transient step.**

What the PDN actually sees is **the derivative of the current**. A di/dt step produces a voltage disturbance V = L·di/dt.
For target impedance, that means:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| ASIC average maximum current | 50 A |
| Transient step ΔI (inside one clock cycle) | 8 A |
| `I_transient` (the one that goes into Z_target) | **8 A**, not 50 A |

If you compute Z_target with 50 A, you get a number **about 6× smaller than the real requirement**.
You will over-stack capacitors. PIPro will draw a PDN that looks perfect —
and the measured ripple will still be large, because the problem lives at higher frequency, where the di/dt is.

## Misreading 2: `V_ripple` is not the datasheet tolerance

The supply tolerance on an ASIC datasheet is usually a **DC + low-frequency** allowance,
something like 1.0 V ± 3%. Feed that 30 mV in as `V_ripple` and the Z_target comes out too loose.

The real V_ripple budget should be:

```text
V_ripple_budget = V_tolerance - V_dc_drop - V_aging - V_other_corner
                = 30 mV    - 10 mV     - 5 mV    - 5 mV
                = 10 mV
```

Which is to say the PDN's true budget for transient movement is **10 mV**, not 30 mV.
A Z_target computed from 30 mV is **3× too optimistic**.

## Putting it together: a small real sum

Suppose:

- ΔI = 10 A (the transient step inside one die)
- V_ripple_budget = 8 mV (the budget that actually exists at the VRM output)

```text
Z_target = 8 mV / 10 A = 0.8 mΩ
```

That number has to hold in the frequency domain from DC out to 1/(2π·t_rise).
If t_rise = 100 ps, then the PDN's |Z| must stay **under 0.8 mΩ from DC to 1.6 GHz**.

That usually means:

- VRM output plus bulk capacitance owns the low band
- The MLCC array owns the middle band
- Package capacitors plus on-die capacitance own the high band
- Miss any one band, and the ASIC sees a transient voltage droop

## What a PIPro run tends to skip

Besides the picture of |Z| against Z_target, a few things deserve a look of their own:

1. **Anti-resonance peaks.** Neighboring capacitors resonate with each other. PIPro draws them by default, and they are easy to ignore
2. **The VRM's output impedance.** A default run often treats the VRM as an ideal short. In fact its output is inductive
3. **Package plus solder balls.** A design whose off-chip PDN looks perfect can be ruined in the high band once the package is included

---

PI design is a **full-band** problem. Miss the target in any one band
and the transient voltage the ASIC sees walks out of the budget.
